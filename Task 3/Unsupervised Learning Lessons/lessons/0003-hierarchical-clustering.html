Hierarchical clustering & dendrograms

The one skill: explain agglomerative clustering in your own words and read a dendrogram.

By the end you can Describe the bottom-up merging process, choose a linkage, plot and cut a dendrogram, and state the trade-offs vs K-Means. This fills your notebook's blank "explain agglomerative" cell and the dendrogram cell.

The idea — bottom-up merging

Agglomerative clustering is the "bottom-up" branch of hierarchical clustering:

  1. Start with every point as its own cluster.
  2. Find the two closest clusters and merge them.
  3. Repeat until everything is one cluster.

The whole merge history is stored as a tree called a dendrogram. Unlike K-Means, you don't have to commit to k up front — you build the tree, then decide where to cut it.

The one-sentence version. Agglomerative clustering repeatedly merges the two closest clusters until one remains, leaving a tree you cut at the height that gives your clusters.

Linkage — how "closest" is defined

Linkage is the rule for measuring distance between two clusters (not between two points):

LinkageCluster distance =Character
ward (default)increase in total within-cluster variance from mergingCompact, even clusters — usually the best default.
completedistance between the farthest pairTight, equal-sized clusters.
averageaverage distance over all pairsBalanced compromise.
singledistance between the closest pairCan chain long, stringy clusters.

Reading a dendrogram

The y-axis is the merge distance, x-axis the points/clusters. To choose k, cut the tree horizontally across the biggest vertical gaps — a tall gap means two clusters were far apart when merged, so that's a natural boundary. Your notebook uses SciPy:

from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import dendrogram, linkage

Z = linkage(data_pca, method='ward')   # the merge history
dendrogram(Z)
plt.xlabel('Samples'); plt.ylabel('Merge distance'); plt.show()

Fitting the model (scikit-learn)

from sklearn.cluster import AgglomerativeClustering

agglo = AgglomerativeClustering(n_clusters=3, linkage='average')
labels = agglo.fit_predict(data_pca)   # one label per row

Note: scikit-learn's version is deterministic — no random_state, no random init. Use distance_threshold instead of n_clusters if you want it to stop merging at a fixed height (then you must also pass n_clusters=None).

Cost. Naively it's O(n²) in time and memory — it computes distances between clusters, so it doesn't scale to millions of rows like K-Means roughly does. Great for small/medium data and for exploring structure.
